It's not "Just" the baby blues, and you're not alone

Motherhood is often described as joyful, but for many new mothers, it also comes with sadness, anxiety, exhaustion, or feeling disconnected from the baby they love. At ORIGEN Healthcare, we provide a compassionate evaluation and honest conversation about what you're experiencing, so we can build a treatment plan that fits your life with a new baby.

We offer psychiatric evaluation, breastfeeding-informed medication guidance, and flexible telehealth visits, because we understand how hard it can be to get to an in-person appointment during the postpartum period.

Postpartum mental health concerns are medical conditions, not a reflection of your ability as a mother. With the right support, most women recover fully.

Why new mothers choose ORIGEN for postpartum care

The postpartum period comes with unique considerations: breastfeeding, sleep deprivation, and simply finding time to get to an appointment. Our approach is built around those realities.

  • Comprehensive psychiatric evaluation for postpartum depression and anxiety
  • Breastfeeding-informed guidance on medication risks and benefits
  • Telehealth visits for both initial and follow-up appointments
  • A safe, non-judgmental space to talk about difficult feelings, including intrusive thoughts
  • In person in Omaha or by telehealth

Baby blues, postpartum depression, or something more?

Not all postpartum emotional changes are the same, and knowing the difference helps guide the right care.

Baby blues

Mild mood swings, tearfulness, and anxiety that typically resolve within two weeks after childbirth.

Postpartum depression

Persistent sadness, hopelessness, or difficulty bonding with your baby that lasts beyond the initial weeks. Affects roughly 15-20% of women.

Postpartum anxiety

Excessive worry, racing thoughts, or physical tension centered on your baby's safety or your ability to cope.

Postpartum psychosis: emergency warning

Postpartum psychosis is rare but a psychiatric emergency. Signs include confusion, seeing or hearing things that are not there, and beliefs that are not true. If you or someone you know has these signs, call 911 or go to the nearest emergency room now. You can also call or text 988 at any time.

Signs you may benefit from postpartum support

If you don't feel more like yourself after the first couple of weeks, it may be more than the baby blues. A consultation with our provider is the best way to understand what you're experiencing and what support could help.

  • Persistent sadness, hopelessness, or crying spells
  • Difficulty bonding with your baby
  • Overwhelming anxiety or racing, intrusive thoughts
  • Withdrawing from your partner, family, or friends
  • Changes in sleep or appetite beyond typical new-parent exhaustion
  • Feeling like you've lost your sense of self

Questions patients ask

What is the difference between baby blues and postpartum depression?

Baby blues are mild mood swings and tearfulness that typically resolve within two weeks after childbirth. Postpartum depression is more severe and persistent, involving ongoing sadness, hopelessness, or difficulty bonding with your baby that lasts beyond the initial weeks.

How common is postpartum depression?

Postpartum depression affects approximately 15 to 20 percent of women after childbirth, making it one of the most common complications of pregnancy and childbirth.

Can I take medication for postpartum depression while breastfeeding?

Many medications are considered compatible with breastfeeding. Your provider will review your specific situation and help you weigh the benefits and risks so you can make an informed decision.

Is postpartum counseling covered by insurance?

Many postpartum evaluation and treatment services are covered by insurance, including medication management. Our team can help verify your specific benefits before your first appointment.

Can partners be involved in postpartum care?

Yes, we welcome a supportive partner or family member to be involved in appointments and treatment planning if that would be helpful to you.

What is postpartum psychosis and how is it different from postpartum depression?

Postpartum psychosis is a rare but serious psychiatric emergency involving symptoms like confusion, hallucinations, or delusions. It requires immediate medical attention and is distinct from the more common postpartum depression and anxiety.
