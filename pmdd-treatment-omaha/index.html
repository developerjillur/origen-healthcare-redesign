What is PMDD?

Premenstrual Dysphoric Disorder (PMDD) is a severe form of premenstrual syndrome involving significant mood symptoms (depression, irritability, anxiety, and mood swings) that occur during the luteal phase (the one to two weeks before your period) and improve once menstruation begins.

The exact cause is unclear, but PMDD is believed to relate to an unusual sensitivity to normal hormonal fluctuations, potentially involving serotonin. It's classified as a mental health condition, but it has a clear biological basis tied to the menstrual cycle.

Because symptoms come and go with the cycle, PMDD is often missed or dismissed as "just PMS." Left untreated, it can significantly affect work, relationships, and quality of life for a meaningful portion of every month.

PMS vs. PMDD: knowing the difference

Not all premenstrual symptoms are the same. Understanding the distinction helps guide the right treatment.

Up to 75%: PMS (Premenstrual syndrome)

Common, typically mild physical and emotional symptoms that don't significantly disrupt work or relationships.

3-8%: PMDD (Premenstrual dysphoric disorder)

Less common but far more severe, with significant mood symptoms that disrupt daily functioning and relationships.

Questions patients ask

What is the difference between PMS and PMDD?

PMS affects up to 75% of menstruating women with typically mild symptoms. PMDD is a more severe condition affecting roughly 3 to 8% of women, with significant mood symptoms like depression, irritability, and anxiety that can disrupt work, school, and relationships.

How is PMDD diagnosed?

PMDD is diagnosed through a clinical evaluation, often supported by tracking your mood symptoms across at least two menstrual cycles to confirm they occur in the luteal phase (the two weeks before your period) and improve once menstruation begins.

What is the first-line treatment for PMDD?

Antidepressants, particularly SSRIs, are typically the first-line medication treatment for moderate to severe PMDD. Some patients take them daily, while others take them only during the luteal phase, depending on their symptoms.

Can PMDD be treated without medication?

Some women find relief through lifestyle changes, exercise, dietary adjustments, and cognitive behavioral therapy, particularly for milder symptoms. Many women benefit from combining these approaches with medication for more significant relief.

Is PMDD treatment covered by insurance?

Many PMDD evaluation and treatment services are covered by insurance, including medication management. Our team can help verify your specific benefits before your first appointment.

Why does PMDD often go undiagnosed?

Because PMDD symptoms are tied to the menstrual cycle and can resemble regular PMS or depression, they are frequently dismissed or misdiagnosed. A mood-tracking evaluation over multiple cycles helps confirm the pattern.
