Personalized ADHD care for every age

Whether it's a child struggling to focus in school or an adult who has managed symptoms quietly for years, ADHD affects daily life in real ways: attention, organization, relationships, and follow-through. What is ADHD?

Attention-Deficit/Hyperactivity Disorder (ADHD) is a neurodevelopmental condition that affects attention, impulse control, and activity levels. It's one of the most common conditions we evaluate and treat, in both children and adults.

ADHD generally falls into three presentations: primarily inattentive (difficulty focusing, following through, or staying organized), primarily hyperactive-impulsive (restlessness, interrupting, acting without thinking), and combined type, which includes both.

Left unaddressed, ADHD can affect school performance, work productivity, and relationships.

Signs of ADHD: children vs. adults

ADHD symptoms can look different depending on age. Recognizing the pattern is the first step toward getting the right evaluation.

In children & teens

Difficulty staying focused on schoolwork; trouble sitting still or waiting their turn; frequently losing homework or belongings; acting without thinking through consequences; struggling to follow multi-step instructions

In adults

Chronic disorganization or missed deadlines; difficulty maintaining focus in meetings or conversations; restlessness or a constant sense of being "on the go"; impulsive decisions in spending, work, or relationships; a lifelong pattern often misattributed to anxiety or stress

Questions patients ask

What are the signs of ADHD in children versus adults?

In children, ADHD often looks like difficulty sitting still, following instructions, or finishing schoolwork. In adults, it more often shows up as trouble organizing tasks, chronic lateness, restlessness, or difficulty focusing at work.

How is ADHD diagnosed?

ADHD is diagnosed through a comprehensive evaluation that reviews symptom history, daily functioning, and in some cases input from family members, teachers, or a partner. There is no single lab test for ADHD.

Does ADHD treatment always involve medication?

No. Treatment plans are individualized and may include medication, behavioral strategies, organizational skills training, or a combination of approaches based on your goals and symptoms.

Is ADHD treatment covered by insurance?

Many ADHD evaluations and treatment visits are covered by insurance. Our team can help verify your specific benefits before your first appointment.

Can adults be diagnosed with ADHD for the first time?

Yes. Many adults are diagnosed later in life after years of managing symptoms without knowing the cause. A psychiatric evaluation can determine whether ADHD is contributing to your symptoms.

Is telehealth available for ADHD appointments?

Yes, we offer both in-person visits at our Omaha office and secure telehealth appointments.

How soon will I notice a difference after starting treatment?

Some medications begin working within days, while behavioral strategies take longer to build. Your provider will monitor your response and adjust your plan as needed.
