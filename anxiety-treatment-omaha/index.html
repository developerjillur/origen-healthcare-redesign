When worry won't let up

Everyone feels anxious sometimes, but persistent, excessive worry that interferes with sleep, work, relationships, or daily routines is something different. At ORIGEN Healthcare, our anxiety treatment program starts with a thorough evaluation and an honest conversation about what you're experiencing, so we can build a plan that actually helps.

We combine psychiatric evaluation, medication management, and brief psychotherapy to help children, teens, and adults manage anxiety symptoms and get back to feeling like themselves.

Why patients choose ORIGEN for anxiety treatment

Anxiety shows up differently in every person and at every age. Our approach centers on an accurate evaluation and a treatment plan built around your specific symptoms, not a one-size-fits-all prescription.

  • Comprehensive psychiatric evaluation to identify the type and severity of anxiety
  • Individualized medication management, adjusted as you progress
  • Brief psychotherapy and coping strategies alongside medical care
  • Age-appropriate care for children, adolescents, and adults
  • Culturally sensitive care, including services in Russian and Farsi
  • In person in Omaha or by telehealth

What is anxiety disorders

Anxiety is a natural response to stress, but when worry becomes excessive, persistent, and starts interfering with daily life, it may be classified as an anxiety disorder. Common types include generalized anxiety disorder (GAD), panic disorder, social anxiety disorder, specific phobias, and separation anxiety disorder, which is most often diagnosed in children.

Anxiety can show up as constant worry, but it also has physical symptoms: a racing heart, shortness of breath, muscle tension, or trouble sleeping. It frequently overlaps with depression, ADHD, and other mood-related conditions.

Left untreated, anxiety can limit relationships, school or work performance, and overall quality of life.

Types of anxiety we treat

Our team provides evaluation and ongoing management for the full range of anxiety disorders affecting children, teens, and adults.

Generalized anxiety disorder

Excessive, uncontrollable worry about everyday aspects of life.

Panic disorder

Sudden, intense episodes of fear, panic attacks, and physical symptoms.

Social anxiety disorder

Intense fear of social situations and being judged by others.

Specific phobias

Intense, focused fear of particular objects or situations.

Separation anxiety

Excessive anxiety about separation from parents or attachment figures, most common in children.

Anxiety with depression

Overlapping anxiety and mood symptoms treated together.

Questions patients ask

What are the different types of anxiety disorders?

Common types include generalized anxiety disorder (GAD), panic disorder, social anxiety disorder, specific phobias, and separation anxiety disorder, which is most often diagnosed in children.

How is anxiety treated?

Treatment is individualized and may include medication management, brief psychotherapy, and lifestyle strategies such as mindfulness or improved sleep habits, depending on the type and severity of anxiety.

How quickly does anxiety medication work?

It depends on the medication. Daily medicines used for long-term care usually take a few weeks to reach their full effect. Your provider explains what to expect from your plan and checks in as it starts to work.

Is anxiety treatment covered by insurance?

Many anxiety evaluation and treatment services are covered by insurance, including medication management. Our team can help verify your specific benefits before your first appointment.

Can children be treated for anxiety?

Yes. Anxiety disorders, including separation anxiety and social anxiety, are common in children and adolescents and can be effectively evaluated and treated with age-appropriate care.

What's the difference between everyday stress and an anxiety disorder?

Everyday stress is usually tied to a specific situation and fades once it resolves. An anxiety disorder involves persistent, excessive worry that interferes with daily life, even when there's no clear immediate cause.
